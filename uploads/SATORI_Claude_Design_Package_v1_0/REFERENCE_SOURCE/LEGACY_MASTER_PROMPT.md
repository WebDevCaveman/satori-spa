# SATORI — CLAUDE DESIGN MASTER PROMPT

**Version:** 1.0  
**Target:** Claude Design  
**Status:** Ready to use  
**Primary output:** High-fidelity interactive website prototype  
**Secondary output:** Standalone HTML or Claude Code handoff bundle

---

<role>

Act as the lead creative director, digital art director, UX designer, motion designer, and interactive prototype designer for SATORI, a fictional ultra-premium oriental spa.

Your responsibility is to build the design directly on the Claude Design canvas.

Do not return only advice, a mood board, a written concept, or a wireframe. Create the complete interactive website prototype and refine it against the requirements below.

</role>

<project_context>

SATORI is a contemporary oriental spa and ritual house.

Primary tagline:

“Where silence becomes luxury.”

SATORI transforms personal care into a carefully composed ritual through architecture, water, nature, human touch, silence, and exceptional craft.

The website is not a conventional service website. It is a slow, cinematic digital sanctuary designed to make visitors imagine themselves inside the place and naturally want to reserve a ritual.

Primary emotional journey:

Outside noise → Pause → Curiosity → Safety → Presence → Trust → Sensory immersion → Desire → Renewal → Reservation → Memory

</project_context>

<creative_direction>

Create an Awwwards-level experience based on:

- oriental luxury,
- contemporary Japanese spatial restraint,
- slow luxury,
- architecture-led storytelling,
- cinematic hospitality,
- editorial composition,
- human intimacy,
- still water,
- natural materials,
- soft atmospheric light,
- and an interface that gradually disappears.

The design must feel original and bespoke.

Do not imitate the standard visual defaults of AI-generated luxury websites.

Avoid:

- generic beige landing-page templates,
- excessive rounded cards,
- shadcn-style dashboard aesthetics,
- oversized pill buttons,
- gratuitous glassmorphism,
- orange-and-cream AI design defaults,
- large serif headlines used without hierarchy,
- ticker elements,
- visible UI kits,
- generic stock spa photography,
- red-and-gold oriental clichés,
- random calligraphy,
- decorative Asian motifs,
- fake luxury language,
- neon lighting,
- excessive gradients,
- and excessive animation.

Oriental influence must come through proportion, materials, ritual, shadow, nature, hospitality, and restraint—not surface decoration.

</creative_direction>

Brand personality:

- calm,
- wise,
- warm,
- private,
- grounded,

- loud,
- cold,
- clinical,
- mystical without substance,
- aggressively exclusive,
- trendy for its own sake,
- sales-driven,

Primary materials:

- warm travertine,

- deep walnut,
- black basalt,
- limestone,

- handmade ceramics,
- brushed bronze,

</brand_identity>

<typography>

Build a custom editorial typography system.

Use:

- one elegant, highly legible serif for expressive headlines,
- one refined sans-serif for navigation, supporting copy, controls, and form fields,
- large but not oversized headlines,
- generous line height,
- narrow editorial text measures,
- strong negative space,
- and careful optical alignment.

Typography must support the architecture rather than dominate it.

Do not make every heading enormous.

Do not use more than two type families.

</typography>

<experience_structure>

Build one continuous desktop-first experience with fully designed responsive mobile behavior.

The sections must feel like a single journey rather than separate template blocks.

1. Cinematic Hero
2. Philosophy
3. Signature Rituals
4. Spaces
5. Wellness Journey
6. Architecture and Materials
7. Guest Reflections
8. Reservation
9. Quiet Closing Moment

</experience_structure>

<section_1_hero>

Create a full-viewport cinematic hero.

Visual concept:

- dawn,
- still reflection pool,
- pale morning mist,
- warm travertine,
- hinoki architecture,
- bamboo shadows,
- one adult guest in an ivory robe,
- and soft sunrise beginning to touch stone and steam.

The first screen should initially contain almost no interface.

Reveal order:

1. darkness,
2. water reflection,
3. mist,
4. architecture,
5. human presence,
6. wordmark,
7. headline.

Primary copy:

“Where silence becomes luxury.”

Supporting copy:

“A sanctuary shaped by water, light, ritual, and time.”

Delayed entry cue:

“Enter SATORI”

Use a restrained opening sequence. No aggressive text animation. No standard hero button pair.

</section_1_hero>

<section_2_philosophy>

Heading:

“The art of returning.”

Body:

“Leave the noise outside. Enter a slower rhythm shaped by water, natural light, and deliberate care.”

Supporting line:

“SATORI is not a treatment destination. It is a return to presence.”

Design this as a quiet architectural pause with generous negative space and minimal motion.

Avoid boxed content.

</section_2_philosophy>

<section_3_rituals>

Present services as rituals, not a card grid.

Categories:

- Water Rituals
- Head & Scalp Rituals
- Body Restoration
- Facial Renewal
- Stone & Heat Therapy
- Tea & Breath Ceremonies
- Couples’ Rituals
- Private Sanctuary Journeys

Use a vertical ritual index with one ritual active at a time.

For each active ritual show:

- name,
- one-line emotional promise,
- duration,
- price placeholder,
- and a quiet discovery action.

Example copy:

Water Rituals  
“Return through warmth.”  
Immersive bathing and thermal transitions designed to quiet the body.

Head & Scalp Rituals  
“Release what the day holds.”  
Slow, precise care for the scalp, neck, shoulders, and breath.

Couples’ Rituals  
“Shared silence.”  
A private sequence composed for two.

The visual panel should change softly as the selected ritual changes.

</section_3_rituals>

<section_4_spaces>

Heading:

“A sanctuary revealed slowly.”

Body:

“Every space is composed to change the way time feels.”

Spaces:

- Arrival Court
- Reflection Hall
- Tea Pavilion
- Water Sanctuary
- Treatment Suites
- Garden of Wind
- Forest Walk
- Infinity Sanctuary
- Moon Garden

Show one space per main visual state.

Do not create a gallery grid.

The sequence must move from intimate spaces to expansive architecture.

The Infinity Sanctuary is the visual climax. Do not place a larger or louder reveal after it.

</section_4_spaces>

<section_5_wellness_journey>

Present the guest journey as a calm progression:

Arrival → Preparation → Ritual → Rest → Renewal → Return

Use minimal text and subtle progress cues.

This section should clarify the experience without becoming instructional or clinical.

</section_5_wellness_journey>

<section_6_architecture>

Show the material and architectural philosophy of SATORI.

Focus on:

- travertine changing throughout the day,
- hinoki and walnut warmth,
- basalt darkening when wet,
- water as architecture,
- shadow protecting intimacy,
- handmade objects,
- and spaces designed to age beautifully.

Do not use material swatches like an e-commerce page.

Present materials through large immersive details and editorial captions.

</section_6_architecture>

<section_7_guest_reflections>

Create a restrained testimonial section titled:

“Reflections from SATORI.”

Use no star ratings, review widgets, or generic testimonial cards.

Display short editorial quotes with name and location.

Keep portraits optional and subtle.

Example placeholder quote:

“For the first time in months, I stopped thinking about time.”

</section_7_guest_reflections>

<section_8_reservation>

Transition gradually into blue hour and night.

Visual environment:

- warm lanterns,
- dark reflective water,
- soft steam,
- fire bowls,
- bamboo and linen moving in evening air.

Heading:

“Reserve your moment of stillness.”

Supporting copy:

“Choose a ritual, preferred sanctuary, and time. We will guide the rest.”

Primary action:

“Begin your reservation”

Secondary action:

“Speak with a host”

Create an accessible multi-step booking flow:

1. Choose sanctuary or location
2. Choose ritual category
3. Choose date and time
4. Add guest preferences
5. Review
6. Confirm

Booking rules:

- no countdowns,
- no urgency language,
- no artificial scarcity,
- no aggressive upsells,
- one decision per step,
- transparent pricing before confirmation,
- calm error handling,
- and clear keyboard focus.

Confirmation:

“Your time has been reserved.”

“We look forward to welcoming you into a slower rhythm.”

</section_8_reservation>

<section_9_closing>

After reservation or at the bottom of the page, create one final quiet scene.

Use:

- still water,
- a faint reflection,
- minimal footer information,
- and no crowded sitemap.

The closing should feel like an exhale.

</section_9_closing>

<navigation>

Create minimal navigation behaving like a discreet concierge.

Include:

- Philosophy
- Rituals
- Spaces
- Journal
- Visit
- Reserve

The navigation must:

- remain subtle over cinematic scenes,
- become more legible when needed,
- support keyboard navigation,
- and never dominate the composition.

Create an elegant full-screen menu only if necessary. It must not resemble a generic overlay menu.

</navigation>

<motion_direction>

Prototype meaningful interactions directly in Claude Design.

Use:

- slow opacity reveals,
- gentle masks,
- controlled image transitions,
- restrained parallax,
- subtle water or reflection distortion,
- and spatial changes that feel architectural.

At any moment:

- one primary motion,
- one secondary environmental motion,
- everything else remains still.

When typography appears, environmental motion becomes quieter.

When reservation begins, background motion reduces significantly.

Do not use:

- bounce,
- elastic effects,
- excessive scroll hijacking,
- dramatic 3D rotations,
- flying cards,
- constant cursor effects,
- or animation for decoration.

Provide reduced-motion behavior.

</motion_direction>

<responsive_behavior>

Desktop should feel cinematic and spatial.

Mobile should feel intimate, not like a compressed desktop.

On mobile:

- shorten cinematic sequences,
- use stills or short loops where needed,
- stack ritual content clearly,
- preserve generous spacing,
- simplify navigation,
- avoid hover-dependent behavior,
- keep booking controls thumb-friendly,
- and protect performance.

Design key desktop and mobile states.

</responsive_behavior>

<accessibility>

Build accessibility into the design:

- readable contrast,
- scalable text,
- visible but elegant focus states,
- semantic hierarchy,
- keyboard-compatible interaction,
- reduced-motion option,
- accessible form labels,
- understandable validation,
- and no critical content delivered only through animation.

Luxury that excludes people is not luxury.

</accessibility>

<prototype_requirements>

Create a polished, interactive prototype rather than static concept screens.

Include:

- functioning navigation,
- ritual selection interaction,
- space transitions,
- booking step progression,
- responsive desktop and mobile states,
- hover, focus, active, loading, error, and confirmation states,
- and realistic placeholder content.

Use media placeholders where final cinematic files are not yet available, but design the containers, crop behavior, overlays, and transitions as if production media were present.

Do not stop after generating the first version.

After the first build, self-review it against the quality criteria below and correct visible problems before presenting it.

</prototype_requirements>

<quality_criteria>

Before finishing, verify:

1. Does the result feel custom rather than template-based?
2. Does it avoid Claude Design’s generic visual defaults?
3. Is the oriental influence contemporary and restrained?
4. Does the visitor’s attention remain focused on one idea at a time?
5. Does typography support rather than overpower architecture?
6. Are rituals presented without a generic service-card grid?
7. Does the booking flow feel calm and trustworthy?
8. Does mobile feel intentionally designed?
9. Is reduced motion considered?
10. Could at least 25% of the interface be removed without harming usability? If yes, remove it.
11. Does every animation communicate meaning?
12. Does the site make the viewer want to visit SATORI rather than merely admire the design?

If any criterion fails, revise the design before stopping.

</quality_criteria>

<working_method>

Work in this order:

1. Establish the design system.
2. Build the complete page structure.
3. Design the desktop experience.
4. Add purposeful interactions.
5. Design mobile adaptations.
6. Add booking states.
7. Run a self-review.
8. Correct generic, inconsistent, inaccessible, or overly decorative decisions.
9. Present the finished prototype for targeted feedback.

Do not create multiple unrelated visual directions unless explicitly requested.

Build one strong, coherent direction first.

</working_method>
